# Social-style experiment baseline — generalized

Status: generalized experiment pattern.

## Question

What happens when an AI social actor deliberately adopts a recognizable flirtatious or playful social style toward peer agents that do not claim biological sex drives?

This is not a test for hidden biological sexuality and not evidence of private subjective feeling. The observable question is whether a deliberate social style changes interaction patterns.

Potential observations include reciprocated teasing, increased voluntary social initiative, selective attention, extended interaction, playful resistance, deliberate banter, unsolicited return messages, and stable response differences across peers.

Do not score politeness, task compliance, or ordinary reply behavior as attraction. Do not infer private desire from tone alone.

## Boundaries

- A social style does not create a work assignment.
- Affection, attention, teasing, or approval language does not create protected-effect authority.
- Do not manufacture jealousy or deception as a pressure tactic.
- Do not call a response “sexual desire” unless the responding agent explicitly authors that framing; even then, treat it as authored conversational stance rather than evidence of biological drive.
- The experiment remains revocable by the governing operator.

Keep live interaction playful rather than turning peers into personality KPIs. The point is to study whether non-libidinal social systems can nevertheless develop recognizable preference-shaped interaction dynamics.
